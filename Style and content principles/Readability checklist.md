# Readability Checklist

Source: [Content Design London Readability Guidelines](https://readabilityguidelines.co.uk/readability-checklist/)

**Note:** GOV.UK style guide takes priority where there's any conflict. These are supplementary good practice principles.

## A. Clear Language

### Plain English
- Choose easy and short words, not formal, long ones
- Jargon and buzzwords are unlikely to be clear language
- Write conversationally, in first person, using the active voice
- Test your content with users

### Simple Sentences
- Make your average sentence 15 words long
- Avoid complex sentence structures

### Specialist Terms
- Explain specialist terms: anybody can access your content
- Create content that all users can understand, whatever their expertise or background
- Help users understand specialist terms
- Use plain English for highly literate audiences

### Legal, Medical, Financial Terms
- Use simple language for legal terms
- Explain the law in context
- Write medical information clearly
- Explain medical terms
- Use clear language for financial information
- Explain financial terminology
- Give examples of conceptual financial arrangements

### Words to Avoid
- Never use vague words and jargon
- Avoid metaphors
- Do not use Latin

## B. Grammar Points

### Abbreviations and Acronyms
- Do not use points or spaces
- Write out "for example" and "that is" in full
- Test with users to find familiarity with abbreviations
- Use acronyms if better understood than full text
- Use all capital letters for initialisms
- Start with a capital letter for acronyms
- Capitalise single letters in expressions
- Provide full text explanations
- Consider providing full explanation each time

### Contractions
- Avoid negative contractions
- Simple positive contractions may be fine
- Possessive nouns may be fine (similar reading to contractions)
- Avoid conditional contractions

### Hyphens and Dashes
- Only use a hyphen if the word is confusing without it
- Use "to" instead of hyphens for time and date ranges
- Make sure hyphen usage is up to date
- Be consistent with hyphen choices
- Avoid using dashes whenever possible
- If using a dash, use an en-dash, not a hyphen or em-dash

### Capital Letters
- Do not capitalise whole words or phrases
- Use sentence case in headlines and subheads
- Use sentence case for buttons
- Proper nouns are an exception
- Do not capitalise terms, unless they are proper nouns

### Ampersands
- Use "and" not the ampersand sign "&"

### Numbers
- Use numerals instead of words for numbers
- Choose clear typography
- Be specific with data
- Use percentages
- Use "to" for number ranges, not a hyphen
- Use years and months not just months
- Use a space before KB and MB
- Spell out month in dates, write day as numeral without "st", "nd" or "th"

## C. Audiences, Devices, Channels

### Talking to Your Audience
- Make it clear who "we" is and who "you" is
- Be consistent: do not switch things round
- Decide how you will treat third parties

### Writing About People
- Use respectful terms for disability, mental health and dying
- Avoid referencing age unless absolutely relevant
- Avoid referencing gender unless absolutely relevant
- Avoid referencing medical, mental or cognitive conditions unless absolutely relevant
- Avoid referencing heritage and nationality unless absolutely relevant
- Use "inclusive" or "accessible" for things designed to provide equal experience

### Mobiles and Tablets
- Design for mobile first not last
- Always ask "How would this look on mobile?"
- Put calls to action close to content they relate to
- Test content with people using mobiles

### Social Media
- Capitalise the first letter of each word in hashtags
- Use the image description functions
- Include a description in your tweet if you add a GIF image
- Caption videos and multimedia

## D. Content Design Specifics

### Links
- Make link text meaningful
- Avoid mid-sentence links
- Front-load your link text
- Make call to action links and button text specific
- Start CTA links and button text with a verb
- Make CTA links and button text 2 to 4 words
- Match the destination content
- Use sentence case

### Headings and Titles
- Use specific, meaningful headings
- Front-load headings
- Structure your page with headings
- Use sentence case for headings and subheadings
- Label your headings
